**Revisions made**

Review of Nov 14 (TK-S97DEW):
- Replace the proximity-only stone check with verified contact -> a stroke now needs the contact itself, read from the simulator's contact solver. The knife's body reports its contacts, and a PhysX rigid-contact view reads the stone's push on the knife after every physics substep. Only the knife-stone pair is read, so the hand and the block don't count. This is the same mechanism the accepted move-live-wire-with-stick task uses. A step counts as on the stone only when the stone pushes the knife with 0.5 N or more AND the geometry holds: the edge is the low side and its lowest point over the stone is within 0.5 mm of its top, so the push is on the edge, not the spine or a face. The knife alone weighs 1.4 N, and the demo pressed a median 2.4 to 3.3 N. New offline checks: the full job with the edge on the stone by geometry but no push, a push under 0.5 N, or no contact reading scores 0; a push with the edge 2 mm up scores 0.
- Run the planned edge-tester calibration (knife, stone, demo pressure and strokes) and adjust the sim thresholds if needed -> the sim's stroke floor is now calibrated against published edge measurements and raised from 10 to 20 per face. Verhoeven ("Experiments on Knife Sharpening", 2004, section 4) hand-sharpened HRC 60 blades "with only a very light downward pressure", as our demo presses (2.4 to 3.3 N, light pressure as Knifewear's guide asks: "just enough weight to maintain contact with the stone"), and examined every edge in a scanning electron microscope: 20 strokes per face removed "all remnants of the original burred edges", while 8 per face were "not adequate". Our thresholds take his 20 as the floor. Where his setup differs from ours, the README notes the direction of each difference:
  - our 1000-grit stone cuts faster per stroke than his 6000 and 8000 grit waterstones (his count is on the safe side for ours);
  - our jointed start needs more steel removed than his burred edges (the real trial's tester reading decides);
  - the demo's press is light, as was his.
  - The real trial stays scored on the edge tester alone, so a robot that strokes 20 times but leaves the edge above 300 g scores 0.5 or less.
- Update the scoring documentation and rerun the full-score demo -> the scoring text in task.json, the README and score() now describes the verified contact and the 20-stroke floor. The demo makes 22 strokes per face in equal alternating sets of 11 (A, B, A, B); at each change of face it lays the knife on the block and grips it again, so the knife's turn in the hand from the stone's drag does not build up. The run limit is 3 minutes (3,600 steps) so the published count fits with time to lay the knife down. Demo: 1.0 in 3,481 steps; Dream-1: PENDING on the same task.py.
- Name every scene item as a real product at its real size -> the stone is a King KW65 1000/6000 (185 x 63 x 25 mm), its holder a Naniwa A-902 (288 x 76 x 36 mm, the stone 32 mm up), the stand under it a Shapton glass-stone field holder base (253 x 80 x 53 mm), and the chopping block a John Boos CCB121203 end-grain maple block (12 x 12 x 3 in), each modeled at the size its maker or a retailer lists and cited in the README's build list. The stone's top is now 11 cm above the bench and the block's 7.6 cm; the demo was re-run on this geometry.

Unchanged: the real-trial ladder (BESS readings at four marked points from a jointed start of 500 g or more), the angle band, the coverage rule and the put-down rule.
