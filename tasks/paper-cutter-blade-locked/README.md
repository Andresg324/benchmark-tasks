# Clear a paper cutter's bed, then lower and latch its blade arm

A guillotine paper cutter is left with its blade arm raised and a stack of trimmed cards on its bed. "Put the paper
cutter away." means taking the cards off the bed and lowering the arm all the way until its safety latch catches.
The arm is spring-balanced: let go before the catch and it swings back up. The cards physically block the arm.

## Files

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `task.py` | Scene, scorer (`score()`, `success()`) and scripted demo (`demo()`), for the `yam_arena` embodiment (Isaac Lab-Arena, bimanual YAM, `station` scene). Its docstrings explain every rule. |
| `yamkit.py` | Helper that spawns the modeled props and the hinged cutter (an articulation with a spring and a catch), and gives the scorer the joint state and the grippers. It patches `yam_arena` in memory at import; no files change. |
| `test_task.py` | Offline scorer checks with hand-made states (near misses included). No simulator needed. |
| `task.json` | The task card. |

## Run a trial

Scripted demo (reaches full score):

    inspect-robots run --embodiment yam_arena --policy yam_scripted -E task=tasks/paper-cutter-blade-locked/task.py \
      -E object_jitter=0 -E control_hz=20 -E cam_width=320 -E cam_height=176 \
      --instruction "Put the paper cutter away." --scorer task_score --max-steps 700

Model run (Dream-1): the same command with `--policy dream --max-steps 2400`.

Render the videos of a run with `inspect-robots video <log.json>`.

Scorer tests: `python tasks/paper-cutter-blade-locked/test_task.py` (prints `all pass`).

## The demo

1. Left arm: grips the card stack across its width, lifts it clear of the bed, draws it toward the robot (away from
   the raised handle) and sets it in the tray, then parks.
2. Right arm: waits, then grips the arm's handle and swings it down along its arc in twelve steps, presses it home
   past the catch, opens and backs off.
3. Both arms return to their starting pose and hold there.

## Scoring

`--scorer task_score` gives 1.0 once `success()` has held for 1 s; otherwise the trial scores `score()` at the end of
the run.

| Outcome at the end | Score |
|---|---|
| Cards off the bed, lying flat on the table or the tray floor and let go, the arm latched and still, both arms home | 1.0 |
| The same, but the arms not back home and still | 0.9 |
| Cards off the bed, arm not latched (still raised, or let go and sprung back) | 0.25 |
| Cards still on the bed (whatever the arm does) | 0 |

Full score also needs both arms back home: each tool point within 3 cm of where it was at the trial's first step,
and still. The arms are put back so the workspace is clear for the next person or job. Set `REQUIRE_ARMS_HOME = False`
at the top of `task.py` to score the job alone. The latch is part of the
mechanism: once it catches it holds the arm down, so it records itself. In a real trial: check the cards are off the
bed and try to lift the arm.

## Runtime and seeds

- Demo: 477 control steps (24 s simulated).
- Model run limit: 2,400 steps (2 minutes at 20 Hz).
- Seed 0, one fixed layout (`object_jitter=0`).

## Fidelity

About 0.7 scale. The cutter is a fixed bed and a hinged arm; the spring is a torque toward raised, and the catch is a
stiff hold applied once the arm is within 1.7 degrees of fully down. Nothing is cut. The cards are one rigid stack.

## Real-world build

A 12-inch office guillotine with a blade lock, its blade guard fitted; a stack of about 50 index cards; any shallow
tray. Mark the cards' start position on the bed with tape.
